**READING GUIDE**

*What this document does*

This working framework answers the decision-making question raised by Aaliyah: what decisions need a vote, what Co-Managing Members can handle, how long members have to respond, and how Meridian decides which deals to take on or exit. This is business-rule language for group review and counsel; it should be conformed to the Operating Agreement before signing.

**PART I**

# Decision Tiers

## 1.1  Core Rule

Meridian should use decision tiers so the group does not have to debate process every time a decision comes up. Routine administration can move quickly. New commitments, deals, member risk, ownership rights, and exit decisions require clearer approval.

While Meridian has four members, Majority means three (3) of four (4) members unless the Operating Agreement defines a different threshold for a specific decision.

## 1.2  Recommended Tier Structure

| Tier | Who Decides | Examples | Approval Rule |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Tier 1: Routine Administration | Co-Managing Member(s) or assigned member | Scheduling, reminders, document collection, advisor coordination, filing routine forms, maintaining records, preparing vote packets. | No separate vote required, but updates should be documented and visible to all members. |
| Tier 2: Approved Budget Execution | Authorized signer(s) after approval | Paying invoices inside an approved budget, signing routine documents for an approved project, ordering inspections or reports already approved in a Deal Approval Memo. | No new vote if the action stays within the approved budget, scope, and deal authority. Payment/signature controls still apply. |
| Tier 3: Group Vote Required | Members | New deal offers, property acquisition, unbudgeted spending, debt, capital calls, member loans, outside investors, material budget changes, sale/refinance, member-vendor transactions, commission treatment, and adding new members. | Majority approval: three (3) of four (4), unless the Operating Agreement requires a higher threshold. |
| Tier 4: Affected Member Written Consent | The affected member | Use of a member's cash, credit, guarantee, member loan, personal collateral, or deal-specific capital. | The affected member must affirmatively approve in writing. Non-response never commits personal money or risk. |
| Tier 5: Core Rights / Unanimous | All members | Changing ownership percentages, changing core voting rights, transfer exceptions, requiring mandatory capital beyond the agreed cap, and any voluntary dissolution rule the members choose to make unanimous. | Unanimous approval: four (4) of four (4), unless the Operating Agreement expressly sets a different rule. |

## 1.3  Adding Members

Adding a new member should be a Tier 3 decision requiring Majority approval. With four current members, that means three (3) of four (4) must approve admission of the new member.

Changing existing members' ownership percentages is different. That should remain a Tier 5 core-rights decision unless all members agree otherwise.

## 1.4  Spending and Signature Distinction

The group should keep approval authority separate from payment-processing authority.

Approval authority means the power to decide whether Meridian takes on a cost, contract, deal, debt, or obligation.

Payment-processing authority means who can sign or release funds after the expense or deal has already been approved.

Recommended working rule: all new expenditures require the applicable member approval first. After approval, one authorized signer may process previously approved budgeted payments under $2,500, two signatures are required for $2,500 to $10,000, and all-member written approval is required above $10,000 or for any unbudgeted expense.

**PART II**

# Response Windows and Non-Response

## 2.1  Written Decision Notice

A member vote should not be valid unless the notice includes enough information for members to make a real decision.

Required notice contents:

* Decision requested.
* Affected deal or company matter.
* Dollar impact and source of funds.
* Required approval threshold.
* Response deadline.
* Consequence of non-response.
* Whether any member cash, credit, guarantee, loan, or personal risk is affected.
* Supporting documents, including the Deal Approval Memo when the vote involves a deal.

## 2.2  Response Deadlines

Recommended working rule:

* Routine decisions: forty-eight (48) hours.
* Urgent deal decisions: twenty-four (24) hours if the notice explains why the matter is urgent.
* Core-rights decisions: no automatic short deadline unless all members agree to use one.

## 2.3  Non-Response

Recommended working rule: if a member does not respond by the stated deadline, the non-response counts as an abstention, not a yes vote.

The decision may proceed only if the required approval threshold is otherwise met.

No member's cash, credit, personal guarantee, member loan, collateral, or deal-specific capital may be committed by silence or non-response.

**PART III**

# Deal Intake and Approval

## 3.1  Deal Filter

Meridian should only spend meaningful time or money on deals that pass a basic screen before a formal vote.

A deal should move forward only if it has:

* A clear property, seller, lead source, or opportunity source.
* A proposed strategy: wholesale, flip, build, hold, refinance, or other.
* A realistic acquisition price or maximum offer range.
* Preliminary numbers showing expected profit, cash need, financing need, and timeline.
* A clear exit path before the offer is made.
* No unresolved title, zoning, permitting, legal, or conflict issue that makes the deal unsuitable.
* A named deal lead responsible for the next step.

## 3.2  Deal Approval Memo

No deal should be considered approved until the members have a written Deal Approval Memo or Deal Participation Agreement.

The memo should include:

* Property address or parcel information.
* Deal source and any related-party/conflict disclosure.
* Strategy and exit plan.
* Maximum offer authority and purchase price.
* Earnest money, due diligence cost, and inspection budget.
* Total project budget, reserves, and expected overrun procedure.
* Financing plan, debt terms, guarantees, and guarantor compensation if any.
* Member cash, credit, loan, guarantee, and role commitments.
* Deal Participation Interests and profit/loss waterfall.
* Vendor plan, including whether a member-owned business will be used.
* Commission treatment.
* Required vote threshold for material changes.
* Go/no-go deadlines and exit triggers.

## 3.3  Deal Approval Stages

| Stage | Decision Point | Required Output | Approval Rule |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1. Intake | Is this worth screening? | Deal lead, source, property, rough strategy, next deadline. | Co-Managing Member(s) or assigned deal lead may prepare the screen. |
| 2. Initial Screen | Does it fit Meridian's strategy and risk? | Rough numbers, red flags, capital need, exit path. | Group discussion before meaningful spend. |
| 3. Offer Approval | Should Meridian submit an offer or LOI? | Deal Approval Memo with max offer, earnest money, funding, roles, and exit plan. | Tier 3 Majority approval, plus Tier 4 consent from any member whose cash, credit, or guarantee is used. |
| 4. Due Diligence Go/No-Go | Should Meridian proceed, renegotiate, or exit before deadlines? | Inspection/title/zoning/budget update and revised deal memo if needed. | Tier 3 Majority approval for proceed/renegotiate; individual consent for personal risk changes. |
| 5. Active Project Changes | Should the budget, timeline, financing, or scope change? | Written change memo explaining variance and recommendation. | Tier 3 vote for material changes; Tier 4 consent for affected member risk. |
| 6. Exit Execution | Should Meridian sell, refinance, hold, assign, or abandon? | Exit memo showing economics, timing, debt payoff, reserves, and distributions. | Tier 3 vote unless the Operating Agreement requires a higher threshold. |

**PART IV**

# Which Deals Meridian Takes On

## 4.1  Recommended Deal Selection Standard

Meridian should take on deals only when the members can answer yes to all of the following:

* The deal fits the company's approved purpose, market, and current strategy.
* The expected profit justifies the time, capital, and risk.
* The financing plan is realistic and does not force any member into unapproved personal exposure.
* The required capital fits within approved member commitments or voluntary deal-specific commitments.
* There is a clear deal lead and role plan.
* There is a documented exit plan and backup exit plan.
* The timeline is realistic for member capacity.
* Conflicts, commissions, and member-vendor relationships are disclosed before approval.

## 4.2  Deal Metrics to Set Before First Offer

The group should choose exact numbers for these items before submitting offers:

| Metric | Recommended Starting Point | Needs Final Group Decision |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Minimum projected profit | Set a minimum dollar profit and/or margin before offers begin. | Yes |
| Maximum required cash per member | Use the Year-1 capital cap or Schedule A capacity, but only with written opt-in. | Yes |
| Maximum personal guarantee exposure | No guarantee without affected member written consent. | Yes |
| Maximum project timeline | Set by strategy: wholesale, flip, build, hold, or refinance. | Yes |
| Budget variance requiring vote | Any material variance; suggested trigger is over $5,000 or over 10% of approved budget. | Yes |
| Required reserve | Reserve should be identified in the Deal Approval Memo. | Yes |

**PART V**

# Exit Strategy and Deal Closeout

## 5.1  Exit Must Be Decided Before Entry

Every deal should have a primary exit strategy and a backup exit strategy before Meridian submits an offer, signs a contract, spends material money, or commits member capital.

Possible exit strategies:

* Wholesale or assignment.
* Renovate and sell.
* Build and sell.
* Hold as rental.
* Refinance and hold.
* Sell as-is if the project no longer fits the numbers.
* Exit during due diligence before earnest money becomes non-refundable.

## 5.2  Exit Review Triggers

The members should revisit the exit plan if any of the following occur:

* Inspection, title, zoning, permitting, lender, or contractor information materially changes the numbers.
* The project budget increases beyond the approved variance threshold.
* The timeline extends beyond the approved project plan.
* Financing terms change.
* A member's cash, credit, guarantee, or role commitment changes.
* The projected profit drops below the approved minimum.
* A better risk-adjusted exit becomes available.

## 5.3  Exit Decision Memo

Before selling, refinancing, holding, assigning, or abandoning a deal, the deal lead should circulate an Exit Decision Memo that includes:

* Recommended exit.
* Current budget-to-actual.
* Debt payoff and closing costs.
* Expected net proceeds.
* Return of capital.
* Preferred return or guarantee premium, if applicable.
* Reserves to hold back.
* Estimated member distributions.
* Risks of choosing the recommended exit.
* Alternative exit options considered.

## 5.4  Deal Closeout

After exit, the Co-Managing Member(s) or deal lead should provide a closeout packet with:

* Final settlement statement or refinance statement.
* Final project budget and variance report.
* Final profit/loss calculation.
* Capital return and distribution calculation.
* Lessons learned.
* Any follow-up tasks for taxes, records, vendors, warranties, or insurance.

**PART VI**

# Items to Put Back Into the Tiebreaker or Operating Agreement

## 6.1  Open Decisions

The group should still make final choices on:

* Exact response windows: 48/24 hours or another structure.
* Whether non-response is abstention or no vote.
* Exact payment-processing thresholds.
* Exact budget variance trigger requiring a new vote.
* Minimum profit or margin required before taking on a deal.
* Maximum project timeline by deal type.
* Required reserves.
* Whether voluntary dissolution requires unanimous approval or another threshold.

## 6.2  Recommended Operating Agreement Language Concept

The Operating Agreement should state that no Company deal is approved unless the members have approved a Deal Approval Memo. The Deal Approval Memo should control deal-specific economics, roles, capital commitments, credit support, guarantees, commission treatment, budget variance rules, and exit plan for that specific deal only.

Equal Company membership should remain separate from deal-level economics. Deal-level economics should follow the actual cash, credit, guarantees, loans, services, roles, and risk approved for that deal.
