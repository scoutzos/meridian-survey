**ARTICLE II**

# Definitions

For purposes of this Agreement, the following capitalized terms have the meanings set forth below. Other capitalized terms are defined where they first appear.

**“Active Deal”** means Any acquisition, project, or transaction the Company has entered into and not yet closed out, including any property under contract, owned by the Company, in renovation, or listed for sale.

**“Capital Account”** means The capital account maintained for each Member in accordance with Article VII and Treasury Regulations § 1.704-1(b)(2)(iv).

**“Capital Contribution”** means Any cash, property, services, or credit/lending capacity contributed by a Member to the Company in accordance with Article IV.

**“Code”** means The Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended.

**“Deal Participation Interest”** means A Member’s share of the economic outcomes (profits, losses, distributions) attributable to a specific deal, calculated based on capital, labor, expertise, and guarantees contributed to that deal. Distinct from Membership Interest.

“Deal Approval Memo” or “Deal Participation Agreement” means the written deal-level agreement required before the Company commits to any acquisition, contract, financing, earnest money deposit, due diligence expense, guarantee, construction budget, or other material obligation for a specific deal. The Deal Approval Memo supplements this Agreement and controls deal-specific economics for that deal only.

**“Decision Packet”** means the written materials circulated to Members before a Tier 3, Tier 4, or Tier 5 decision, including the requested decision, required approval threshold, response deadline, dollar impact, affected Member risk, and supporting documents.

**“Exit Decision Memo”** means the written deal-level memorandum required before the Company sells, refinances, holds, assigns, abandons, or otherwise exits a deal, as described in Section 9.7.

**“Fair Market Value”** means The price at which property would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, neither under compulsion, both with reasonable knowledge of relevant facts. Where this Agreement requires a determination of Fair Market Value and the parties cannot agree, an independent appraiser mutually selected (or, failing agreement within thirty (30) days, appointed by the American Arbitration Association) shall determine value, with cost split equally.

**“Managing Member”** means A Member designated under Section 5.1 to perform day-to-day management functions on behalf of the Company.

**“Member”** means Each person or entity admitted as a Member of the Company in accordance with this Agreement.

**“Membership Interest”** means A Member’s ownership interest in the Company itself, expressed as a percentage. Membership Interest determines voting rights (subject to Article V), share of losses (subject to Article VI), and share of residual assets on dissolution. Membership Interests are equal among the four initial Members unless amended under this Agreement.

**“Majority”** means the affirmative vote of more than fifty percent (50%) of the Members entitled to vote on the matter. With four (4) Members, Majority means three (3) affirmative votes unless this Agreement provides otherwise.

**“Supermajority”** means the affirmative vote of at least seventy-five percent (75%) of the Members entitled to vote on the matter. With four (4) Members, Supermajority means three (3) affirmative votes in the normal course; where a vote excludes a Member (e.g., on that Member’s removal), Supermajority shall mean three (3) affirmative votes of the remaining three (3) Members.

**“Unanimous”** means The affirmative vote or written consent of all Members.

**ARTICLE III**

# Membership and Membership Interests

## **3.1  Membership Interests**

Each of the four initial Members holds an equal one-fourth (1/4) Membership Interest in the Company. For accounting purposes where decimal expression is required, each Member's interest shall be expressed as twenty-five percent (25.00%). Membership Interests govern entity-level ownership and are distinct from the Deal Participation Interests defined in Section 3.2, which govern the economics of specific deals. Membership Interests are fixed unless adjusted by amendment under Article XXII or by operation of Section 4.3 (late or non-contributing Members).

*(Updated for the current four-member structure. Deal-level economics remain separate from Company-level Membership Interests.)*

**3.2  Deal Participation Interests**

For each deal pursued by the Company, the participating Members shall agree, before the deal is approved, on each Member’s Deal Participation Interest — the percentage of that deal’s profits, losses, and distributions allocable to such Member, based on capital, labor, expertise, and guarantees contributed to that specific deal. Deal Participation Interests apply only to economics of the specific deal and do not alter Membership Interests.

Deal Participation Interests for each deal shall be documented in a Deal Approval Memo signed by the participating Members and retained in the Company records (Article XIII).

No Company deal shall be treated as approved unless the Members have approved a Deal Approval Memo or Deal Participation Agreement describing, at minimum: (a) the property or parcel; (b) strategy and exit plan; (c) purchase price or offer authority; (d) earnest money and due diligence budget; (e) capital contributions, credit support, guarantees, and member loans; (f) roles, responsibilities, management fees, commissions, and vendor conflicts; (g) Deal Participation Interests and profit/loss waterfall; (h) capital-call or overrun procedure; (i) required vote threshold for material changes; and (j) go/no-go deadlines.  
Equal Membership Interests in the Company do not require equal economics on every deal. Deal-level economics shall be determined by the approved Deal Approval Memo for that specific opportunity, based on documented cash, credit, guarantees, services, expertise, and risk assumed by the participating Members.  
(Added to clarify the two-layer structure: Company-level rules in this Agreement; deal-level economics and roles in a separate deal agreement.)

New Members may be admitted only with Majority approval of existing Members. While the Company has four (4) Members, Majority approval means written consent of at least three (3) existing Members. Changing the Membership Interests or core voting rights of existing Members is not an admission decision and remains subject to the Unanimous approval requirement for core provisions under Article XXII.

**ARTICLE VI**

# Profits, Distributions, and Losses

## **6.1  Allocations Generally**

Allocations of profits, losses, and items of income, gain, deduction, and credit shall be made in a manner intended to comply with Section 704(b) of the Code and the Treasury Regulations thereunder, and to have substantial economic effect. The Members intend that the allocations described in this Article VI produce final Capital Account balances that, on liquidation, equal the amounts the Members would receive under the distribution waterfall in Section 6.2.

If at any time the Company’s tax accountant determines that the allocations herein fail to satisfy IRC § 704(b), the Members shall amend this Article VI by majority vote to bring the allocations into compliance, with the goal of preserving the economic deal among the Members.

## **6.2  Profit Distribution**

Profits from each deal shall be distributed in the following order (a deal-by-deal waterfall):

1. **Repayment of Credit Obligations.** Members who contributed credit or lending capacity to the deal — including those whose personal guarantees enabled the financing — shall be repaid the agreed value of that contribution in full before any other distribution. Outstanding loan balances secured by such guarantees shall be retired or refinanced as a first priority.  
2. **Return of Capital.** Cash and property contributors receive a return of capital contributed to the deal.  
3. **Preferred Return.** 8% preferred return paid to capital contributors on contributed capital.  
4. **Reserve.** 10% of remaining net profit retained by the Company as operating reserve.  
5. **Remaining Profit.** Split per the Deal Participation Interests for that deal as documented under Section 3.2.

*(Retained from the original majority-aligned draft; credit-first priority added per group decision.)*

## **6.3  Distribution Timing**

Distributions shall be made within thirty (30) days after each deal closes, once all deal-related obligations have been satisfied and reserves have been funded. Quarterly reconciliations of reserves and outstanding obligations shall be performed.

*(Retained from the original majority-aligned draft.)*

## **6.4  Loss Allocation**

Losses shall be allocated to the Members in proportion to their Deal Participation Interests for the deal that gave rise to the loss; Company-level losses not attributable to a specific deal shall be allocated pro rata by Membership Interest, in each case subject to the override in Section 6.1 (704(b) compliance).

*(Retained from the original majority-aligned draft; refined here to align profit and loss allocation with deal-level economics, per CPA best practice.)*

## **6.5  Reserves**

The Company shall maintain operating reserves funded by the 10% retention in Section 6.2(3) and by any additional amounts the Members determine necessary by majority vote. Reserves shall be held in a designated Company account separate from deal-specific funds.

## **8.8  Incapacitation**

This Section governs the rights and obligations of the Members and the Company in the event a Member becomes incapacitated. Heirs and beneficiaries play no role during a Member’s incapacitation; their rights, if any, arise only upon a Member’s death under Section 8.7.

Designated Representative. Each Member shall deliver to the Company a durable power of attorney designating an individual (the “Designated Representative”) to act on the Member’s behalf in matters concerning the Member’s interest in the Company in the event of the Member’s incapacitation. The durable power of attorney shall be delivered within thirty (30) days following execution of this Agreement and updated within thirty (30) days following any change in the Designated Representative. Failure to deliver or maintain a current durable power of attorney shall be deemed a failure to perform agreed-upon responsibilities under Section 8.6(c) and subject to the cure rights therein. The Company may, at its discretion, require disclosure of the executed durable power of attorney document upon a Member’s incapacitation.

Definition of Incapacitation. For purposes of this Agreement, a Member is “incapacitated” upon the certification, in writing, of two (2) licensed physicians — at least one of whom shall be independent of the Member’s family — that the Member is unable, by reason of physical or mental condition, to fulfill the Member’s duties under this Agreement for the foreseeable future. The Company shall bear the cost of the independent physician’s certification.

Grace Period. Upon a determination of incapacitation, a six (6) month grace period shall apply, during which: (a) the Designated Representative may act on the incapacitated Member’s behalf; (b) the incapacitated Member shall remain a Member with all economic rights to distributions, profit allocations, and Capital Account balances; and (c) the voting authority of the Designated Representative shall be limited as set forth in the Decision Required block immediately below.

**DECISION REQUIRED — Voting scope of the Designated Representative during the grace period**

The Designated Representative is the Member’s POA holder — not themselves a Member of the Company. The group must decide how much voting authority that person carries on behalf of the incapacitated Member during the grace period.

**Option A:**  Full voting rights. Designated Representative votes on all matters as the Member would. Most respectful of the Member’s POA designation; introduces a non-Member into operational decisions.

**Option B:**  Economic-only. Designated Representative votes only on matters affecting the Member’s economic position (distributions, buyouts, valuations, indemnification). Operational, strategic, new-deal, amendment, and dissolution votes proceed without the incapacitated Member’s vote (counted as abstaining). Protects partnership integrity.

**Option C:**  Reduced quorum and threshold. Designated Representative has full voting rights, but the incapacitated Member’s vote is excluded from quorum and from supermajority/unanimous threshold calculations during the grace period (i.e., votes are calculated on the basis of the non-incapacitated Members only).

***Working Default (in effect until group finalizes):**  Option B. Designated Representative may act on economic and protective matters concerning the incapacitated Member’s interest; operational, strategic, and amendment votes proceed without the incapacitated Member’s vote, which is counted as abstaining for threshold purposes.*

Mandatory Buyout Right. If the Member has not been certified by two licensed physicians as having recovered sufficiently to resume duties within six (6) months following the determination of incapacitation, the Company shall have the right to exercise a mandatory buyout of the incapacitated Member’s interest at Fair Market Value, in accordance with Sections 8.3 (Valuation) and 8.5 (Payment Timeline). The buyout shall be exercised, if at all, by Supermajority vote of the remaining Members within ninety (90) days following the six-month anniversary of incapacitation. Payment shall be made to the Member, or to the Designated Representative on the Member’s behalf, or to a court-appointed conservator if one has been appointed.

Permanent Incapacitation. If, before the expiration of the six-month grace period, two (2) licensed physicians certify in writing that the Member’s incapacitation is permanent and that the Member will not be able to resume duties, the Company may, by Supermajority vote of the remaining Members, exercise the buyout right immediately upon such certification, without waiting for the six-month period to elapse.

Partial Recovery. A Member who recovers during the grace period may resume duties upon delivery of a written certification by two (2) licensed physicians that the Member is fit to do so. Voting and management rights shall be restored upon resumption of duties; full participation rights (including in deals approved during the grace period) shall be restored after ninety (90) days of consistent participation, with deal-level economics for that period determined by the Members in good faith.

Death During Incapacitation. If an incapacitated Member dies before resolution of the grace period, the Member’s interest shall be treated under Section 8.7 (Death of a Member) rather than this Section 8.8. Any partial buyout payments already made under this Section shall be credited against the death-buyout obligation.

Absence of Designated Representative. If a Member becomes incapacitated and no current durable power of attorney is on file with the Company, the remaining Members may, by Supermajority vote, appoint a temporary representative to act in matters strictly necessary to protect the incapacitated Member’s economic interest until either a Designated Representative is identified, a court-appointed conservator is appointed, or the buyout right under this Section is exercised. The temporary representative shall not vote on operational, strategic, or amendment matters.

**DECISION REQUIRED — Disability buy-sell insurance**

The Section 8.8 buyout obligation can require the Company to fund a substantial payment on short notice. Disability buy-sell insurance funds this obligation, parallel to term life insurance recommended in Section 8.7 for the death buyout.

**Option A:**  Required. The Company shall purchase and maintain disability buy-sell insurance on each Member, in an amount sized to approximate the estimated buyout obligation. Premiums are a Company expense. Strongest funding posture; highest ongoing cost.

**Option B:**  Required after first deal closing. The Company obtains disability insurance once Year-1 capital is committed and the buyout exposure becomes meaningful. Defers cost until risk materializes.

**Option C:**  Recommended, not required. The Company evaluates disability insurance at its annual insurance review (Section 14.2) and may purchase by majority vote. Most flexible; weakest funding.

***Working Default (in effect until group finalizes):**  Option B. The Co-Managing Members shall obtain disability buy-sell insurance quotes within sixty (60) days following the closing of the first Company deal, and the Members shall finalize coverage by majority vote within thirty (30) days thereafter.*

*(Retained from the original majority-aligned draft on the six-month grace concept; expanded here to address POA mechanics, definition of incapacitation, scope of representative authority, partial recovery, death-during-incapacitation, and funding via disability insurance.)*

**ARTICLE IX**

# Financing and Debt

## **9.1  Use of Leverage**

The Company shall use leverage to maximize returns. The preferred strategy is hard money lending for fix-and-flip projects (maximum 90% loan-to-value) and conventional financing for buy-and-hold assets (maximum 80% loan-to-value). All debt over $25,000 requires Supermajority approval.

*(Retained from the original majority-aligned draft; current four-member responses support both leverage and the hard-money/conventional strategy mix.)*

## **9.2  Personal Guarantees**

**ADOPTED WORKING TERM — Equal guarantee exposure principle**

Current four-member survey result: three (3) of four (4) current Members selected that all Members should guarantee Company debt equally, while one Member selected no personal guarantees. Because a personal guarantee cannot be imposed by silence or general authority, the operating rule is equal-exposure protection whenever guarantees are used, subject to each guarantor's affirmative written approval in the applicable Deal Approval Memo.

In practice, lenders require guarantees from Members who qualify; the framework below operationalizes the equal-exposure principle:

* Members with credit profiles meeting the lender’s minimum threshold (the “Guarantor Pool”) shall be expected to provide personal guarantees as required by the lender on a given deal.

* Guarantor Pool Members shall receive a three percent (3%) guarantee premium on the deal’s net profits, paid before the residual split under Section 6.2(4).

* Members not in the Guarantor Pool may participate in the deal but shall have their Deal Participation Interest reduced proportionally to reflect the absence of guarantee risk; reduction percentages set deal-by-deal at the time of approval.

* No Member shall be required to guarantee a debt that, at the time of guarantee, would exceed twenty-five percent (25%) of that Member’s net worth.

*(Retained from the original majority-aligned draft; framework added to make it operationally workable with the realities of lender credit requirements.)*

## **9.3  Member Loans**

A Member may, by majority approval of the other Members, lend funds to the Company on terms negotiated at the time of the loan. Member loans are debt obligations, not Capital Contributions, and shall be documented by promissory note with interest rate, maturity, and security (if any) specified. Member loans repaid before equity distributions under Article VI.

## **9.4  Deal Selection Standard**

The Company shall take on deals only when the Members can reasonably determine, based on the information then available, that the deal fits the Company's approved purpose, market, current strategy, and risk tolerance; the expected profit justifies the time, capital, and risk; the financing plan is realistic; no Member is forced into unapproved personal exposure; the required capital fits within approved Member commitments or voluntary deal-specific commitments; there is a clear deal lead and role plan; there is a documented primary exit plan and backup exit plan; the timeline is realistic for Member capacity; and conflicts, commissions, and member-vendor relationships are disclosed before approval.

## **9.5  Deal Approval Stages**

Unless the Members approve a different written process for a specific opportunity, Company deals shall move through the following stages:

| Stage | Decision Point | Required Output | Approval Rule |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1. Intake | Is this worth screening? | Deal lead, source, property, rough strategy, and next deadline. | Co-Managing Member(s) or assigned deal lead may prepare the screen. |
| 2. Initial Screen | Does it fit Meridian's strategy and risk? | Rough numbers, red flags, capital need, financing need, and exit path. | Group discussion before meaningful Company spend. |
| 3. Offer Approval | Should Meridian submit an offer or letter of intent? | Deal Approval Memo with maximum offer, earnest money, funding, roles, and exit plan. | Tier 3 Majority approval, plus Tier 4 written consent from any Member whose cash, credit, guarantee, member loan, or collateral is used. |
| 4. Due Diligence Go/No-Go | Should Meridian proceed, renegotiate, or exit before deadlines? | Inspection, title, zoning, budget, financing, and revised deal-memo update if needed. | Tier 3 Majority approval for proceed or renegotiate; Tier 4 written consent for changed personal risk. |
| 5. Active Project Changes | Should budget, timeline, financing, scope, or roles change? | Written change memo explaining variance and recommendation. | Tier 3 Majority approval for material changes; Tier 4 written consent for affected Member risk. |
| 6. Exit Execution | Should Meridian sell, refinance, hold, assign, or abandon? | Exit Decision Memo showing economics, timing, debt payoff, reserves, and distributions. | Tier 3 Majority approval unless this Agreement requires a higher threshold. |

## **9.6  Deal Approval Memo**

No deal is approved until the Members have approved a Deal Approval Memo or Deal Participation Agreement meeting the requirements of Section 3.2 and Schedule C. The Deal Approval Memo controls deal-specific economics, roles, capital commitments, credit support, guarantees, member loans, commission treatment, budget variance rules, material-change thresholds, and exit plan for that deal only. Equal Membership Interests in the Company do not require equal economics on every deal.

## **9.7  Exit Strategy and Exit Decision Memo**

Every deal must have a primary exit strategy and backup exit strategy before the Company submits an offer, signs a contract, spends material money, or commits Member capital. Possible exit strategies include wholesale or assignment, renovate and sell, build and sell, hold as rental, refinance and hold, sell as-is if the project no longer fits the numbers, or exit during due diligence before earnest money becomes non-refundable.

The Members shall revisit the exit plan if inspection, title, zoning, permitting, lender, contractor, budget, timeline, financing, role, or Member-risk information materially changes the approved deal assumptions; if projected profit drops below the approved minimum; or if a better risk-adjusted exit becomes available.

Before selling, refinancing, holding, assigning, or abandoning a deal, the deal lead shall circulate an Exit Decision Memo that includes the recommended exit, current budget-to-actual, debt payoff and closing costs, expected net proceeds, return of capital, preferred return or guarantee premium if applicable, reserves to hold back, estimated Member distributions, risks of the recommended exit, and alternative exit options considered.

## **9.8  Deal Closeout**

After each deal exits, the Co-Managing Member(s) or deal lead shall provide a closeout packet with the final settlement statement or refinance statement, final project budget and variance report, final profit/loss calculation, capital return and distribution calculation, lessons learned, and follow-up tasks for taxes, records, vendors, warranties, insurance, or other post-closing matters.
